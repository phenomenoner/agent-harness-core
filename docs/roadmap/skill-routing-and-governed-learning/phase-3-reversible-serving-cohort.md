# Phase 3 — Reversible Serving Cohort

- **Status:** planned
- **Depends on:** accepted shadow-routing comparison, frozen serving candidate,
  and accepted progress-surface and terminal-authority contracts
- **Unlocks:** outcome-governed skill improvement through the
  [Governed Learning Loop](phase-4-governed-learning-loop.md)

## Outcome

One exact, low-risk cohort receives a measured role-first routing or disclosure
improvement. A single control restores the prior serving policy without data
conversion, revision drift, or provider ambiguity.

## Purpose in the Whole Project

Shadow evidence reduces uncertainty but does not improve a user's task. This
phase is the first controlled conversion of skill intelligence into user-visible
value.

Its purpose is also architectural: it proves that the harness can freeze a
virtual session's catalog and skill revisions, deliver minimal procedural
content, preserve continuity across backend-session rollover, and fall back
without creating a second serving authority.

## Starting Point

The shadow-routing work supplies an accepted policy revision, comparison
report, exact outcome join, and one proposed cohort. The Phase 1 reliability
foundation supplies the provider-surface and causal-latency revisions that the
cohort must preserve. Current serving remains the fallback. No learning or
skill mutation is enabled by this phase.

## Scope

### In scope

- one exact cohort expressed with existing identity and authority boundaries;
- a small, explicitly listed set of low-risk skills and competence roles;
- a version-bound serving policy and catalog;
- virtual-session manifests that freeze selected revisions and disclosure;
- minimal sufficient disclosure and stable rehydration across concrete-session
  rollover;
- feature-gated activation, monitoring, circuit breaking, fallback, and exact
  readback;
- end-to-end outcome, cost, and provider evidence for the cohort.

### Out of scope

- global activation;
- skill-body generation, mutation, or promotion;
- broad cohort discovery;
- high-risk or privileged procedures without their own authority gates;
- a queue or provider-adapter rewrite;
- background consolidation or scheduled reflection.

## Implementation Plan

### 1. Freeze the serving candidate

Bind the policy revision, catalog digest, skill revisions, cohort expression,
expected benefit, cost budget, and fallback policy. Any behavioral change after
freeze creates a new candidate and invalidates prior acceptance evidence.

### 2. Enforce one serving owner

Place the policy choice at the existing prompt-assembly authority. Shadow mode
may observe, but cannot also disclose. Backend capability negotiation must
prevent double selection or double injection when native skill support exists.

### 3. Freeze virtual-session revisions

Create a manifest containing the exact catalog, topology, selected revisions,
and disclosed fragments. Concrete backend sessions may resume, compact, or roll
over without silently changing those bytes. A receipted delta is allowed only
for an explicit material task change or safety quarantine.

### 4. Implement cohort activation and fallback

Activate only the exact cohort. Unknown identity, missing revision, failed
receipt, stale policy, or unavailable fallback leaves current serving in
control. The fallback switch must not require catalog or state conversion.

### 5. Observe quality, cost, and safety

Measure held-out outcome, verifier evidence, correction, disclosure bytes,
latency, tokens, retries, conflicts, and unknown rate. Apply circuit breakers to
forbidden selection, cross-identity evidence, provider duplication, cost
overrun, or material quality regression.

### 6. Perform bounded rollout

Run offline and staged scenarios before authorized provider-visible smokes.
Expand only within the frozen cohort after the prior ring passes. Preserve an
exact cohort-level rollback and readback record.

## Acceptance Plan

| Gate | Required evidence | Minimum level |
|---|---|---|
| Single serving authority | Exactly one policy supplies prompt skill content; native backend capability negotiation prevents duplicate disclosure | T3 |
| Frozen continuity | Resume, compaction, rollover, and catalog changes do not drift an active virtual session | T3 |
| Clean task boundary | A new task starts a new manifest without stale task intent or skill deltas | T3 |
| Exact cohort isolation | Near-match agents, accounts, channels, users, or sessions remain on the prior policy | T3 |
| User value | Held-out cohort outcomes improve, or cost falls without material quality loss, against the frozen shadow-routing baseline | T3 plus measured cohort |
| Fallback | One control restores prior serving, active-session behavior follows the declared policy, and readback proves the result | T3 |
| Provider delivery | Exact-lane Telegram and Discord acknowledgement, progress update, terminal uniqueness, send/edit receipt, readback, retry, and duplicate-suppression checks for each supported adapter affected by activation | T4 smoke |
| Resource bounds | Prompt bytes, latency, evaluator load, retries, and evidence growth stay within the frozen budget | T3/T4 measurement |

Provider smoke is evidence only for the exact tested lane and policy revision.
It does not prove global provider health or endurance.

## Acceptance Artifacts

- frozen cohort and serving-candidate manifest;
- feature and fallback policy;
- virtual-session revision manifest and delta contract;
- offline and staged replay report;
- exact-lane provider smoke receipts in private evidence;
- cohort outcome and cost comparison;
- rollback and post-rollback readback;
- phase acceptance record.

## Public Explanation at Acceptance

Public text must name the serving behavior that changed, the exact supported
cohort and providers, the measured outcome or cost effect, and the exercised
fallback. It must also name untested cohorts, providers, platforms, and
endurance limits. "Serving rollout complete" cannot imply a global deployment.

## Transition from Shadow Routing and Attribution

Do not change comparison metrics, corpus labels, catalog semantics, or the
expected benefit after seeing cohort results. If those inputs change, return to
side-effect-free routing comparison and issue a new serving candidate.

## Transition to the Governed Learning Loop

The learning-loop work may learn only from accepted, exact-cohort outcomes
whose delivered revisions and evidence quality are known. It must not treat
selection, exposure, or missing feedback as positive evidence. Keep serving and
mutation controls separate so a learning failure cannot disable the proven
cohort.

## Stop and Rollback Conditions

Immediately fall back on cross-identity selection, forbidden or conflicting
skill delivery, duplicate provider effects, manifest drift, inability to read
the active revision, rollback uncertainty, or material quality or cost
regression. Preserve receipts before classification and return changed routing
behavior to side-effect-free comparison.
